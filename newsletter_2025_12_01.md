# LLMs in Evidence Synthesis - December 01, 2025

Welcome to your weekly update on Large Language Models in Evidence Synthesis. Here's what's happening.

## New Tool/App

### M-Reason: Multi-agent System for Biomedical Evidence Synthesis
**Relevance:** 9/10 | **Models:** 

M-Reason is a system that uses large language models and modular agent orchestration to automate evidence retrieval, appraisal, and synthesis in the biomedical domain. It emphasizes explainability and user auditability, providing traceability from source evidence to conclusions, and demonstrates efficiency gains in evidence synthesis tasks.

### REASON: LLM Pipeline for Evidence Synthesis
**Relevance:** 9/10 | **Models:** 

The paper introduces REASON, an end-to-end agent-based pipeline that utilizes large language models for evidence synthesis. It incorporates retrieval, classification, and structured summarization to enhance the evidence assessment process.

### Multi-Agent LLM Framework for Clinical Trial Data Extraction
**Relevance:** 9/10 | **Models:** GPT-4, Claude 3.5, Llama 3.1

The paper presents a multi-agent LLM framework that automates the extraction and analysis of clinical trial data, utilizing models like GPT-4, Claude 3.5, and Llama 3.1. This method aims to accelerate systematic reviews and enhance evidence synthesis by employing structured JSON extraction.

### Interactive Platform for AI-Powered Literature Synthesis
**Relevance:** 8/10 | **Models:** 

The paper introduces the Atlas of Human-AI Interaction, a web interface that uses LLM-powered knowledge extraction to map empirical findings from HCI literature. It identifies causal relationships and visualizes them as a knowledge graph, aiding in the discovery of research gaps and evidence-based design.

## Methodology

### Evaluating LLMs for CONSORT Adherence in RCTs
**Relevance:** 8/10 | **Models:** Gemini-2.5-Flash, DeepSeek-R1, GPT-4o

This study evaluates the performance of large language models in identifying adherence to CONSORT reporting guidelines in randomized controlled trials. The findings indicate that while LLMs can accurately identify compliant items, they struggle with non-compliant and not applicable items, limiting their current utility in replacing human expertise for trial quality appraisal.

---

### Assessing Demographic Bias in LLMs for Systematic Review Summaries
**Relevance:** 8/10 | **Models:** Qwen, Longformer, GPT-4.1 Nano

The paper evaluates the ability of language models to retain age-related information in biomedical evidence synthesis, using a novel dataset called DemogSummary. It introduces the Demographic Salience Score (DSS) to assess the fidelity of age-related entity retention in summaries generated by LLMs like Qwen, Longformer, and GPT-4.1 Nano.

---

### MARAG-R1: Multi-Tool Retrieval for Enhanced Evidence Synthesis
**Relevance:** 8/10 | **Models:** 

The paper introduces MARAG-R1, a framework that enhances Retrieval-Augmented Generation by using multiple retrieval tools to improve information access for evidence synthesis. This method employs reinforcement learning to dynamically coordinate retrieval mechanisms, enabling more comprehensive corpus-level reasoning.

---

### Model-Document Protocol for Enhanced LLM Document Interaction
**Relevance:** 8/10 | **Models:** 

The paper introduces the Model-Document Protocol (MDP), a framework that transforms unstructured documents into LLM-ready inputs through structured pathways. MDP-Agent, an instantiation of this protocol, demonstrates improved performance in information-seeking tasks by synthesizing large-scale evidence into coherent context.

**Relevance:** 8/10 | **Models:** 

The paper discusses the development of an agentic retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system for synthesizing structured evidence into coherent outputs. This method leverages LLMs to enhance the accuracy and contextual relevance of evidence synthesis tasks.
