SLRAI Wizards: AI Tool for Systematic Literature Reviews

Models: ChatGPT
The paper introduces SLRAI Wizards, a ChatGPT-based platform designed to assist in conducting systematic literature reviews (SLRs). The tool guides users through planning, conducting, and reporting SLRs using established frameworks like PRISMA and CASP. It offers three engagement modes and features such as a Screening Wizard for rapid abstract processing. Preliminary evaluations show positive user feedback, indicating increased confidence and satisfaction in conducting AI-assisted SLRs.

MetaBeeAI: AI Pipeline for Systematic Reviews in Biology

MetaBeeAI is an open-source, modular pipeline designed to facilitate systematic reviews and meta-analyses in the biological sciences using Large Language Models (LLMs). It addresses the challenge of extracting and synthesizing information from complex and voluminous scientific literature. The tool includes an intuitive interface for human oversight, allowing users to inspect and correct model outputs. Evaluated on 924 research papers, MetaBeeAI demonstrates its capability to handle heterogeneous experimental designs and contexts, providing a scalable and transparent framework for evidence synthesis.

Programmable Framework for Automated Risk-of-Bias Assessment

Models: Mistral Small 3.1, GPT-oss-20b, GPT-5 Nano, GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.5 Sonnet
This study introduces a programmable framework for automating risk-of-bias (RoB) assessment in randomized controlled trials using large language models (LLMs). The method employs DSPy and its GEPA module to replace manual prompt design with structured, code-based optimization, enhancing reproducibility and transparency. Evaluated on 100 RCTs, GEPA-generated prompts showed superior performance in several RoB domains compared to manually designed prompts, demonstrating the potential for consistent and reproducible RoB assessments in evidence synthesis.

LLM Literature Review Assistant Design and Evaluation

This study introduces a web-based tool utilizing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to assist students in writing literature reviews. The tool allows users to upload academic papers, draft reviews, and receive feedback from a Large Language Model (LLM). A user study with ten postgraduate students demonstrated measurable improvements in writing skills, particularly in citation guidance, structure, and synthesis. The findings indicate that LLM-powered tools can effectively support the development of writing skills in academic settings.

AI Pipeline for Knowledge Extraction in Hyperaccumulation Studies

The paper presents an AI-based pipeline designed to automate the extraction of structured knowledge from scientific literature on heavy metal hyperaccumulation in plants. This tool converts unstructured data, including text, tables, and figures, into a structured database, enabling rapid dataset generation. The system was validated by replicating a manually curated dataset, achieving similar accuracy in a fraction of the time. It incorporates a dual-validation strategy with a 'LLM-as-a-Judge' layer to ensure contextual correctness, highlighting the importance of semantic validation in data extraction.

Prospective Evaluation of NeutrinoReview Screening Algorithms

Models: LLaMA 3.1-8B, GPT-4
This paper presents a protocol for the prospective evaluation of LLM-based screening algorithms integrated into NeutrinoReview, a web application designed to automate the screening phase of systematic reviews. The study evaluates two algorithms, 5-Tier and CAL-X, which leverage LLMs to prioritize high-sensitivity pre-filtration of studies, aiming to reduce the workload in evidence synthesis. Preliminary results indicate workload reductions between 36% and 72%, with further assessments pending human consensus.

Evaluating ChatGPT-4o for Risk of Bias and Data Extraction

Models: ChatGPT-4o
This study evaluates the performance of ChatGPT-4o in assessing risk of bias using the RoB 2.0 tool and extracting data from randomized controlled trials on exercise therapy for chronic low back pain. The tool showed limited reliability in bias assessment, likely due to the interpretative complexity of the task, but performed robustly in data extraction with high accuracy and F1-scores. The findings suggest that while ChatGPT-4o may not yet be suitable for evaluative tasks requiring critical judgment, it has potential as a secondary reviewer in systematic review workflows, particularly for data extraction tasks.

LLM-Based Literature Classification for Learning Support

Models: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Owen-7B, LLaMA3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B
This study proposes a literature classification scheme using large language models (LLMs) to automate the classification of learning support literature. The method involves a two-stage experiment with three open-source LLMs, including DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Owen-7B, LLaMA3.1-8B, and Qwen2.5-7B, to evaluate different classification strategies. The LLaMA-3.1-8B model demonstrated the best performance after optimization, achieving high F1 values for both Chinese and English literature. This approach shows potential for automating literature classification in social science research.

Review/Benchmark

Benchmarking Deep Research Agents for Report Synthesis

This paper introduces FINDER, a benchmark designed to evaluate Deep Research Agents (DRAs) on their ability to generate comprehensive research reports. FINDER includes 100 human-curated tasks with 419 checklist items to standardize report structure and analytical depth. Additionally, the paper presents the Deep rEsearch Failure Taxonomy (DEFT), identifying 14 failure modes in DRAs related to reasoning, retrieval, and evidence integration. The study highlights that while DRAs understand tasks, they struggle with integrating and verifying evidence, which is crucial for effective report synthesis.
