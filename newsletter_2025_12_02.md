## New Tool/App

### SLRAI Wizards: AI Tool for Systematic Literature Reviews
**Models:** ChatGPT

The paper introduces SLRAI Wizards, a ChatGPT-based platform designed to assist in conducting systematic literature reviews (SLRs). It addresses the challenge of complex methodologies and fragmented tools by providing a comprehensive solution that guides users through planning, conducting, and reporting SLRs using established frameworks like PRISMA and CASP. The tool offers three engagement modes and features such as a Screening Wizard for rapid abstract processing. Preliminary evaluations show positive user perceptions, indicating increased confidence and satisfaction in conducting AI-assisted SLRs.

### MetaBeeAI: Modular Pipeline for Systematic Reviews
MetaBeeAI is an open-source, modular pipeline designed to facilitate systematic reviews and meta-analyses in the biological sciences using Large Language Models (LLMs). It addresses the challenge of extracting and synthesizing information from complex scientific literature by providing a scalable and transparent solution. The tool includes an intuitive interface for human oversight, allowing users to inspect and correct model outputs. Evaluated on 924 research papers, MetaBeeAI demonstrates its capability to handle heterogeneous experimental designs and contexts, supporting reliable replication and continual refinement.

🔗 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.24.690154) | [Link](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.24.690154)

### Programmable Framework for Automated Risk-of-Bias Assessment
**Models:** Mistral Small 3.1, GPT-oss-20b, GPT-5 Nano, GPT-5 Mini, Claude 3.5 Sonnet

This study introduces a programmable pipeline for automating risk-of-bias (RoB) assessment in randomized controlled trials using large language models (LLMs). The method employs DSPy's GEPA module to replace manual prompt design with structured, code-based optimization, enhancing reproducibility and transparency. Evaluated on 100 RCTs, GEPA-generated prompts showed superior performance in domains like Random Sequence Generation, outperforming manual prompts by 30%-40% in accuracy. The study demonstrates GEPA's potential to streamline RoB assessment, supporting evidence synthesis with consistent and reproducible results.

🔗 [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01452v1)

### LLM Literature Review Assistant for Students
This study introduces a web-based tool utilizing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to assist students in writing literature reviews. The tool allows users to upload academic papers, draft reviews, and receive feedback from a Large Language Model (LLM). A user study with ten postgraduate students demonstrated measurable improvements in writing skills, particularly in citation guidance, structure, and synthesis. The findings indicate that LLM-powered tools can effectively support the development of writing skills in academic settings.

🔗 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.65106/apubs.2025.2703) | [Link](https://doi.org/10.65106/apubs.2025.2703)

### AI Pipeline for Knowledge Extraction in Hyperaccumulation Studies
The paper presents an AI-based pipeline designed to automate the extraction of structured knowledge from scientific literature on heavy metal hyperaccumulation in plants. This tool converts unstructured data, including text, tables, and figures, into a structured database, enabling rapid dataset generation. The system was validated by replicating a manually curated dataset, achieving similar accuracy in a fraction of the time. It incorporates a dual-validation strategy with a 'LLM-as-a-Judge' layer to ensure contextual correctness, highlighting the importance of semantic validation in data extraction.

🔗 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/make7040152) | [Link](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-4990/7/4/152/pdf?version=1764080429)

## Methodology

### Prospective Evaluation of NeutrinoReview Screening Algorithms
**Models:** LLaMA 3.1-8B, GPT-4

This paper presents a protocol for the prospective evaluation of LLM-based screening algorithms integrated into the NeutrinoReview web application. The study focuses on two algorithms, 5-Tier and CAL-X, which leverage LLMs to function as pre-filtration systems in systematic reviews, aiming to reduce workload by prioritizing high sensitivity. Preliminary results indicate workload reductions between 36% and 72%, with further human consensus assessments pending. The LLM used in this study is LLaMA 3.1-8B, hosted at CERN.

🔗 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17554629)

### Evaluating ChatGPT-4o for Risk of Bias and Data Extraction
**Models:** ChatGPT-4o

This study evaluates the performance of ChatGPT-4o in assessing risk of bias using the RoB 2.0 tool and extracting data from randomized controlled trials on exercise therapy for chronic low back pain. The tool showed limited reliability in bias assessment, likely due to the interpretative complexity of the task, but demonstrated strong performance in data extraction with accuracy above 84% and F1-scores over 90%. The study suggests that while ChatGPT-4o may not yet be suitable for evaluative tasks requiring critical judgment, it has potential as a secondary reviewer in systematic review workflows, particularly for data extraction tasks.

🔗 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.4081/reumatismo.2025.2224) | [Link](https://doi.org/10.4081/reumatismo.2025.2224)

### LLMs for Automated Literature Classification in LIS
**Models:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Owen-7B, LLaMA3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B

This study proposes a literature classification scheme using large language models (LLMs) to automate the classification of learning support literature. The method involves a two-stage experiment with three open-source LLMs, including DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Owen-7B, LLaMA3.1-8B, and Qwen2.5-7B, to evaluate different classification strategies. The LLaMA-3.1-8B model showed the best performance after optimization, achieving high F1 values for both Chinese and English literature. This approach demonstrates the feasibility of using LLMs to support social science research by automating literature classification tasks.

🔗 [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1108/el-03-2025-0099)
